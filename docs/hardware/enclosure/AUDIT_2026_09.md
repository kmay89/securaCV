# Enclosure audit, 2026-09 — findings, what was fixed, what is still open

A design audit of all 44 enclosure `.scad` sources across eleven dimensions:
ribs and stiffening, drop and impact, weather and ingress, openings and
bridges, clearance stacks, printability, assembly, repairability, parametric
UX, and aesthetics.

**The rule of evidence, and it is the point of this document.** Every finding
below was proved by rendering and measuring — an `admesh` section, a wafer
probe, or an assembled intersection judged by
[`canary_s3_lcd7_fitcheck.scad`](./canary_s3_lcd7_fitcheck.scad)'s recipe. A
comment is not evidence. Where a claim rests on reading the source rather than
running it, it says so.

This is a dated record, not a live tracker. Fixed rows stay, because the
evidence for *why* a number is what it is now is the most useful thing in here.

---

## What the audit found about the catalog as a whole

Three patterns explain most of the individual findings.

**1. Rules were written and not enforced.** `canary_core_lib.scad` carried
`wall_rib()` — with a docstring that is a drop specification, "a 2 mm PETG wall
longer than ~60 mm oil-cans under a thumb and rings when dropped" — and
`grep -rn wall_rib *.scad` returned only its own definition. Zero adopters in
44 files, while released shells ran 70.6 to 107.6 mm of bare wall. The same
shape of gap covered the rib root fillet (one file has them, ~25 sites want
them), the radome's quarter-wave guard (an assert in one file, a comment in the
two that copied it), and the house look.

**2. A copy is a fork, and the forks had already drifted.** The corner-post
gusset is hand-copied six times, and the four released copies clamp the gusset
height while the two development copies dropped the clamp. The board rail is
eleven typed `cube([3, ...])`s, none of which consults the floor it stands on.
The catalog names this failure mode itself — *a lesson applied to a copy stays
in that copy* — and it was live in three places at once.

**3. Single-part gates cannot see assembly defects.** The most serious finding
here (CLR-1) is four watertight, single-part, warning-free meshes that do not
go together. `render.sh` was green, `admesh` was green, and the interference
was fully buried. Only putting two parts together finds that class, and only
the 7" case had a gate for it.

## Open — blocking

These are proved and **not** fixed here. Each is a part that does not work.

### `canary_hub_din` — the cover cannot close
Two independent probes found it. The cover's screw-guide tubes are
`ch - 2 = 20.0 mm` tall and drive straight through the F-F standoffs they are
meant to meet: **8.99 mm of interference**, measured. Separately (CLR-4) the
cover has no Z datum at all — 2.0 mm of free travel, closing by landing on the
bare Pi PCB — and the file's only fastener statement, "M3 x 25", is wrong in
both diameter and length (the screws are M2.5).

Also on this part: four Ø5.2 × 20 mm tubes with a 1.2 mm wall stand unbraced on
a lid that is 44 % grating. 20 / 1.2 = 16.7, past the slenderness ceiling
`canary_rib_lib` now defines — `boss_tower()` exists for exactly this and would
taper and web them.

### `canary_vision_enclosure` — the hooded weather front has no printable orientation
A **released STL**. Found independently by the printability and the openings
audits, with agreeing numbers: hood-up leaves 32.66 mm² of first layer (four
Ø3.59 post tips) under a 16.8 mm part with 4,061.99 mm² of dead-flat
unsupported ceiling including a 48 × 81 mm slab at z = 0; face-down is no
better at 68.12 mm² / 4,109.71 mm². The README and the Cura cheat-sheet name
*opposite* poses, and neither works. A fix is a real design change — a
separately-printed or bridged hood — not a parameter.

### `canary_relay_solar` — the pole pod has no drain
Its only floor opening is the USB port the documentation tells you to plug. The
solar roof's panel bed is an undrained gutter with a 2 mm dam across its low
edge; the SMA bulkhead is an unhooded upward-facing penetration on the sky
wall; and the ePTFE vent seat is cut on the **outer** face (the comment says
inner), making a 0.9 mm cup on the membrane. The declared 110 mm panel also
collides with the pod (265 mm³) and blocks the SMA.

### `canary_field_case` — the lens bezel stands proud of the impact boot
The only part in the catalog whose design *intent* is a drop rating. The bezel
stands 0.2 mm proud of the TPU boot rim, so a lid-face drop lands the whole
case on a press-fit trim ring over the lens. `batt_h` and `foam_t` are dead
knobs: the pack floats 4.0 mm, and an over-tall pack decompresses the O-ring
with no gate. Worst O-ring clamp span is 47.1 mm, not the header's 41, against
the project's own ≤ 40 mm rule.

## Open — major, by theme

**Stiffening.** The `canary_rib_lib` adopters are listed in its header and none
have landed yet. The highest-value: the Hammond chassis plate (158 × 88 × 2.4 mm
with nothing on its long axis, and it is the structural member of the one build
whose shell is a *bought* IP66 rating); the dash display's back (113.7 × 73.6 ×
2.4 mm, and four snap-dock pads on it are assumed coplanar by a gate that can
see a bad span but not a bad plane); the sign; the gang wallplate. The field
case's battery divider stops 0.5 mm short of **both** side walls — probe-proven
void — so the drop case's only transverse member ties nothing.

**Ribs proportioned backwards.** `lid_rib_w = 2.5` on a `lid_t` of 2.0–2.2 in
three files, with `lid_rib_h = 1.0`: w/t 1.25 and h/w 0.40. Stiffness goes as
h³, so this buys 1.66× rather than the README's "roughly trebling", and a
4.0 mm-tall rib was probe-proven to clear at zero envelope cost. The doorbell's
±Y corner gussets render as an **empty object** — the target circle is
concentric with the post — so that case has no gussets at all despite the code.

**Weather.** The released WAP weather base's gasket cheeks are 0.80 mm, half
what its three siblings were corrected to. The groove-fill arithmetic lives in
a comment, and `gasket_w > 2.5` overfills the groove and jacks the lid open
rather than sealing. Nothing asserts that a sealed build has any pressure path
at all. The doorbell's "blind" security-screw pilot becomes a through-hole into
the cavity whenever `wall_eff < 2.4`.

**Openings.** `port_bridge_profile2d()`'s chamfer is a constant 2.5, so the
residual flat span is unbounded and unasserted — the dash's 24 mm terminal
opening leaves a 19.00 mm flat bridge through the same call. The 1.69" case's
USB opening clears the shell and blocks the cable: the plug's overmold is
stopped 4.25 mm early, latching on 2.25 mm of its 6.5 mm insertion.

**Assembly and service.** `screw_insert` reaches four files, all released; six
screwed cases self-tap printed plastic with no insert path — including the
doorbell's security screw, which is undone at every service. Nothing in the
catalog counts hardware, and the one header BOM quantity has already drifted.
The watch station, C6 and 1.69" snap closed on a flush Ø-for-Ø parting line
with no pry point, release or grip (Ø49.000 vs Ø49.000, probed).

**Parametric UX.** The full spec is in the audit report; the headline counts,
machine-derived with the catalog's own parser: 705 of 1704 parameters have no
help at all, and **174 of those have help that was written and is mechanically
discarded** (125 to multi-assignment lines, 49 to comments placed above the
assignment) — the cheapest fixes in the audit. 1436 of 1461 numbers are
free-text boxes; the whole catalog has 25 sliders. The "two-stud wall-hanging
interface" the README says transfers between parts appears under **14 different
group names** across 17 files. Only two of the released four have a preset, and
the two without have the most unexplained controls (46 and 61).

**Naming collisions that make a transferred skill actively wrong.** `usb_w` /
`usb_h` mean *the opening in the wall* in the case files and *the connector
shell plus a fit clearance* in the display cases; `vm_*` is a Grove board in
three files and a radar carrier in two; `skirt_t` is a rain skirt in five and a
bezel snap finger in one; `clip_w` is 6.0 or 45.0 depending on the file.

## What was checked and found clean

Recorded so the next pass need not redo it.

- No rib crosses or shortens a snap beam anywhere in the catalog — checked
  specifically on the DIN leaf, the watch-station bezel fingers, the
  1.69"/C6 skirt nubs and the 1.47" band.
- The WAP's `batt_hold` genuinely lands: the lid rib's face sits exactly
  `batt_h` above the cavity floor, preserving the 1 mm swelling allowance.
- The WAP's corner screw posts are gusseted to **both** adjacent walls; the
  free-standing-tower failure mode is not present on any released lid post.
- The doorbell's cavity corner is a real R8.0 — its pill outline saves it from
  DR-2.
- The dash display's **frame** is fine: the face rim looks floppy in isolation
  but 3.5 × 16.6 mm walls rise off it all round, so it is a deep L-section.
- The field case's divider proportions (0.44× the floor, 4:1 height-to-
  thickness) are correct; only its ends are wrong.
- The Sense's 54.2 mm and the WAP compact's 29.7 mm wall spans are inside the
  60 mm panel limit and want no ribs.

## What the audit did not reach

`canary_s3_lcd7.scad` (4,897 lines) got no aesthetic pass and no rendered
verification of its claimed root fillets — its statements here are *read*, not
proven, which is the same trap this document is otherwise written against.
`canary_c3_lcd147.scad` (2,060 lines) was only grepped. No quantitative
drop-energy arithmetic was done anywhere: every drop finding is geometric —
thickness, span, notch radius, free travel, load-path continuity — measured off
the meshes. **Nothing here is a rated drop height, and per the house rules none
of it should be read as one.** A rating is design intent, earned per printed
unit, never asserted from the CAD.
