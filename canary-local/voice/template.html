The Canary has a voice.

Every alert and interaction on the display is one square-wave piezo — but shaped so a note can breathe, chirp, and trill. These are its acoustic signatures, drawn from a warm pentatonic so they're a family, with the intruder alarm held deliberately apart. The pad isn't populated on hardware yet, so this is the first place anyone can hear them — and every sound here is generated from the firmware, not hand-copied, so it can't drift.

Faithful to the firmware by construction: every frame you hear and every duty on the meters is rendered from voice_score.h by the real compiler, then played back verbatim — the preview owns no audio math of its own. Interaction and ambient voices go silent at night; the alarm keeps a floor at every volume, including Off. The browser adds a gentle low-pass where the piezo's own physics would; the exact timbre waits for the bench.
