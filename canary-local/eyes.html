Through Canary eyes

On a Canary Vision, the camera module hands the firmware boxes — six integers per moving thing — and the firmware never sees a pixel. This bench puts your webcam on the sensor side of that boundary: a stand-in motion sensor stages the boxes, and the real firmware — the same detection filter, voxel tracker and presence engine the device runs, compiled to WebAssembly — makes every call after it. Walk into frame and watch yourself become a box, a voxel, and a presence event. Never a picture.

Honest by construction: the person class, score threshold, timeouts and voxel grid shown here are read from the running firmware, and every verdict — is someone present, are they dwelling, which event fires — is made by firmware/projects/canary-vision's detect_config.cpp, voxel_tracker.cpp and presence_fsm.cpp compiled to WebAssembly, the same sources the Vision bench drives with staged actors and CI rebuilds and byte-diffs. Your camera is the only new input; its frames are examined in this page and never recorded, stored, or sent anywhere — only boxes cross the sensor boundary, exactly as on hardware. The stand-in sensor is a motion detector, humbler than the real module. This is a demonstration of how the presence engine behaves — not a security device.
