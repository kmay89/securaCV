What a listening Canary hears

One Canary in the line has a microphone — the Dash on the Waveshare 4.3C. It listens for the cadence a smoke or CO alarm sounds, and — if you opt in — a sudden loud onset (a door) to wake the screen. It never listens for words. This bench runs that exact decision core — the firmware's own logic, ported 1:1 to JavaScript and re-pinned to the header in CI — so you can watch what it does, and prove what it doesn't, before you own a board.

Honest by construction: every number on this page — the sensitivity presets, the T3/T4 beep windows, the wake threshold — is read from the same constants the firmware compiles, and the verdict (is this a loud onset, does this rhythm fit the grammar, does an Alert fire) is made by a 1:1 JavaScript port of firmware/projects/canary-display/…/mic_logic.h, which canary-local/tests/mic.test.js re-pins against the firmware header and replays the host test's own scenarios through, every CI run. In the synthetic mode nothing but a script drives it; in live mode your microphone is the only new input, and only one loudness number per 20 ms ever crosses into the detector — the samples behind it are averaged to it and erased, never recorded, stored, or sent. This is a demonstration of how the detector behaves — not a smoke or CO alarm, and no substitute for a UL-listed one.
